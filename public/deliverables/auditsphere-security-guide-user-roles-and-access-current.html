AuditSphere Roles & Access — End-User Guide

This guide explains every user role in AuditSphere, what each role can and cannot do, how separation of duties works, and how to tell us which work email addresses should hold each role.

1. What we need from you

Action Required: After reading this 10-minute guide, please reply with the exact Microsoft sign-in work email address for each person in your firm or client finance team who needs access, using the 9-column request table in Section 9 (or the companion CSV file auditsphere-security-template-role-assignment-request.csv).

  • Where to send your completed list: [insert recipient email / secure onboarding inbox, e.g., auditsphere-access@example.com]
  • Target reply deadline: [insert target date, e.g., DD Month YYYY by 17:00]
  • Non-negotiable separation-of-duties rule: Every person who prepares work (audit procedures, trial-balance mappings, adjustment journals, financial packages, or firm invoices) must have a different person assigned to review or approve that work. One person cannot approve their own preparation, even if granted two roles.

2. 60-second overview

AuditSphere separates internal staff delivery from external client collaboration across two distinct portals:

  • Staff Workspace (/app): For internal firm partners, managers, auditors, client accountants, commercial relationship managers, firm finance staff, and administrators.
  • Client Portal (/portal): For external client finance contacts (ClientUser) to upload Provided by Client (PBC) audit documents, reply to queries, and respond to proposed management adjustment journals and financial packages.

Everyone signs in with their Microsoft work account (Microsoft Entra ID). Signing in proves identity, but opens nothing until an AuditSphere Administrator records a Role Grant combining a Role (what they do) and a Scope (which clients, engagements, or consolidation groups they may access).

3. Role plus scope

Every access grant pairs a Role (who the person is and what actions they can perform) with a Scope (where they are permitted to act):

Scope Code Plain-English Meaning Example Strict Boundary (What It Never Covers)
FIRM_WIDE Across your firm's eligible portfolio for that role. Required for firm administration, firm ledger books, and CRM pipeline management. Amira holds Partner at FIRM_WIDE; Omar holds Administrator at FIRM_WIDE. Never allowed for ClientUser. Does not grant other roles or cross-firm access.
CLIENT One specific client company and its engagements (where the operation accepts client-level scope). Tariq holds Manager on Client: Gulf Horizon Trading LLC. Cannot view or act on any sibling client company or firm-wide ledger/admin screens.
ENGAGEMENT One specific audit or accounting engagement. The server resolves the parent client link from stored database records. Lina holds Senior on Engagement: Gulf Horizon FY2026 Statutory Audit. Cannot see other engagements of the same client (such as FY2025 Audit or Tax Advisory).
GROUP One corporate consolidation group via a dedicated GroupAccessGrant. Youssef holds AccountingPreparer on Group: Gulf Horizon Holdings Group. Cannot have an expiry date; forbidden for Senior, RelationshipManager, and ClientUser; does not open standalone subsidiary client books.

6. Approvals and separation of duties

  • Dual-Key Acceptance + Paid 50% Advance (ADR-0003 & ADR-0012): Engagement activation and Client Portal access require (1) Key 1 client commercial acceptance of the exact proposal revision, (2) Key 2 unconditional risk clearance by the staffed ENGAGEMENT_PARTNER, and (3) the 50% ADVANCE billing milestone marked PAID.
  • Preparer Is Never Reviewer (Maker-Checker): Across audit procedures, conditional task exclusions, client accounting mappings/adjustments/reconciliations/packages/consolidations, and firm invoices/ledger journals, the server checks PreparedByUserId != ReviewerUserId. Assigning two roles to one user never bypasses the two-person rule.
  • Partner-Only Authority & Independent EQR: Only a Partner can clear Key 2 risk, activate engagements, approve high-risk procedures, verify signed LORs, sign opinions/reports, or release financial packages. When EQR applies, the EQR Partner must be a different Partner from the Engagement Partner.
  • Administrator Is Not Partner: Administrator access provides user management and 13 audited read-only oversight views, but never confers Partner, FinanceManager, or FinanceReviewer authority.

8. Which role should this person have?

Follow these 5 questions from top to bottom to pick the right role for each person:

  1. Q1: Do they work for the external client company? → Yes: ClientUser (CLIENT or ENGAGEMENT). No: go to Q2.
  2. Q2: Do they run platform administration, commercial CRM, or the firm's own internal billing/ledger? → User access & settings: Administrator | Leads & proposals: RelationshipManager | Preparing firm invoices/ledger: FinanceManager | Approving firm invoices/ledger: FinanceReviewer. Otherwise go to Q3.
  3. Q3: Do they focus strictly on client accounting (R2R) or Group Consolidation without performing audit fieldwork? → Preparing mappings, adjustments, statements, or consolidations: AccountingPreparer | Independently reviewing/approving them: AccountingReviewer. Otherwise go to Q4.
  4. Q4: Do they lead engagements, manage staffing, or sign off reviews? → Signing opinions, Key 2 risk clearance, activation & EQR: Partner | Staffing, planning & Rank 3 review: Manager. Otherwise go to Q5.
  5. Q5: Do they coordinate fieldwork and perform Rank 2 review, or prepare assigned workpapers? → Fieldwork lead & Rank 2 review: Senior | Preparing assigned workpapers & TB intake: Staff.

10. What happens after you reply

  1. Request received: Your Partner or Administrator checks role/scope alignment and confirms maker-checker pairs.
  2. Administrator verifies identity: Searches Users & Access to check whether the user already exists.
  3. Identity bound or invited: Uses Bind a roster identity to pre-register the user's exact Microsoft sign-in email, display name, and account type (STAFF or CLIENT).
  4. Role and scope assigned: Saves the grant in Assign role and scope, writing an immutable RoleGrantAuditEvent and generating a copyable invitation link.
  5. First sign-in: The user signs in with their Microsoft work account (and if ClientUser, accepts security terms PORTAL-SECURITY-2026-1 once the 4 onboarding gates are met).
  6. Confirmation: The Administrator verifies the active grant and confirms completion back to you. (Note: Administrator turnaround SLA and invitation link TTL are marked TO CONFIRM in Appendix B.)

11. Changing or removing access

  • When someone leaves or rotates off an engagement: Email your Administrator with the person's name, work email, whether to revoke a single role/scope grant or disable their entire account, and the reason/approver.
  • Automatic Expiry (RoleGrantExpiry): When ExpiresAt passes on a FIRM_WIDE, CLIENT, or ENGAGEMENT grant, the server automatically marks it revoked with Source = "EXPIRY" and NewRole = "EXPIRED" during session evaluation.
  • Immediate Session Invalidation (SessionEpoch): Every grant change, expiry, revocation, or account disablement increments AppUser.SessionEpoch, immediately invalidating any open browser session on the next request.
  • Immutable Audit History (RoleGrantAuditEvent): Revoking access never deletes past working paper sign-offs or audit history; all grant changes remain permanently recorded in RoleGrantAuditEvents.

12. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) and Glossary

Frequently Asked Questions

  1. Can one person hold multiple roles? Yes, across the same or different scopes—but holding two roles never permits self-review.
  2. Does Administrator include Partner or FinanceReviewer authority? No. Administrator access never confers Partner, FinanceManager, or FinanceReviewer authority.
  3. Why is the Client Portal locked right after granting ClientUser? It opens only after Key 1 acceptance, Key 2 Partner risk clearance, the 50% advance milestone marked PAID, and Partner activation.
  4. Can a ClientUser delegate a PBC request? Yes—if they are the request owner and the client's primary contact, they can delegate a PBC request to another active ClientUser of the same client.
  5. What if a commercial proposal is revised after Key 1 acceptance? Key 1 is bound to the exact proposal revision; a new revision requires re-acceptance.
  6. Can a GROUP grant have an expiry date? No; RoleAssignmentService blocks ExpiresAt on GROUP grants.
  7. Can we schedule a future start date for a grant? No; EffectiveFrom more than 1 minute in the future is rejected by the server.
  8. Does CLIENT scope grant access to a parent consolidation GROUP? No; group consolidation requires a separate GROUP grant (or FIRM_WIDE where accepted).
  9. How do Role Grants relate to Engagement Staffing Ranks? Security Role Grants open routes/capabilities; Engagement Staffing (ENGAGEMENT_PARTNER rank 4 down to STAFF_ASSOCIATE rank 1) governs engagement procedure review thresholds and Key 2 risk clearance.
  10. Are Microsoft Graph write permissions required? No; optional M365 write capabilities are disabled by default, and local Bind a roster identity works without them.
